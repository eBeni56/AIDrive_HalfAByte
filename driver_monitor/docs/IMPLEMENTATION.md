# driver_monitor — what it detects, how, and where the code is

Camera-based driver monitoring built on **MediaPipe Face Landmarker** (478 face landmarks incl. iris,
52 blendshapes, head-pose matrix). Every camera frame produces one stateful JSON message
(`driver_state`) describing the driver's eyes, head, gaze, mouth, attention and drowsiness, plus a list
of **detections** (labelled episodes such as `MICROSLEEP` or `DISTRACTED`) with stable ids, start times,
durations, severities and type-specific details.

Emotions are deliberately **not** included. The JSON format field by field (for consumers): [detection-schema.md](detection-schema.md).

## 1. What it can detect

| Detection | Meaning | Main method | Severity | Code |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `EYES_CLOSED` | Eyes shut longer than a blink (≥ 0.5 s) | Fused eye openness + hysteresis state + timer | WARNING | [detectors/eye_closure.py](../src/driver_monitor/detectors/eye_closure.py) |
| `MICROSLEEP` | Eyes shut ≥ 1.0 s | same; escalates from `EYES_CLOSED` | CRITICAL | [detectors/eye_closure.py](../src/driver_monitor/detectors/eye_closure.py) |
| `EYES_ALMOST_CLOSED` | Eyes held nearly shut ≥ 2 s (heavy eyelids) | Eye state `ALMOST_CLOSED` + guards (look-down, smile, yawn) | WARNING | [detectors/almost_closed.py](../src/driver_monitor/detectors/almost_closed.py) |
| `EYES_NARROWED` | Eyes open but clearly narrower than **this driver's normal** for ≥ 5 s (early tiredness) | 3 s median openness ÷ the personal normal opening learned after calibration (ratio < 0.85) | INFO → WARNING | [detectors/narrowed.py](../src/driver_monitor/detectors/narrowed.py), [state/eye_baseline.py](../src/driver_monitor/state/eye_baseline.py) |
| `TIRED_EYES` | Heavy eyelids / slow blinks / many long blinks over 60 s | Rolling eye statistics | INFO → WARNING | [detectors/tired_eyes.py](../src/driver_monitor/detectors/tired_eyes.py) |
| `YAWN` | One continuous mouth opening: wide ≥ 0.4 s, or moderate ≥ 1.5 s (natural yawns) | Inner-lip MAR + `jawOpen` (ignored with the head down) + timer | INFO | [detectors/yawn.py](../src/driver_monitor/detectors/yawn.py) |
| `HEAD_NOD` | Quick drowsy head drop and catch | Pitch drop speed/size from a normal position + recovery + eyes **measured closed** during it | WARNING / DANGER | [detectors/head_nod.py](../src/driver_monitor/detectors/head_nod.py) |
| `HEAD_FALLING` | Head sinking forward / slumping sideways / tipping back **with closed eyes** | Head-pose history (held angle or steady drift) + eye closure; never with open eyes, never with the head turned > 30° | DANGER / CRITICAL | [detectors/head_falling.py](../src/driver_monitor/detectors/head_falling.py) |
| `DISTRACTED` | Not paying attention: looking away too long. **Down** (phone, lap) ≥ 1.2 s; sideways/up or staring at a mirror ≥ 3 s; mirror checks are fine | Gaze (head + eyes) → zone (road, 3 mirrors, side, up, down), per-zone glance timers + weighted cumulative timer | WARNING / DANGER | [detectors/distraction.py](../src/driver_monitor/detectors/distraction.py) |
| `FACE_NOT_VISIBLE` | Face lost ≥ 2 s (fail-safe), with a guessed cause | Tracking loss + last known pose | WARNING / DANGER | [detectors/face_visibility.py](../src/driver_monitor/detectors/face_visibility.py) |
| `DROWSY` | Overall tiredness level (5 levels) that builds up over minutes and recovers | 60 s eye statistics + decaying fatigue load of past events (every closure ≥ 0.5 s weighted by duration, nods, falls, yawns) | INFO … CRITICAL | [state/drowsiness.py](../src/driver_monitor/state/drowsiness.py), [state/fatigue.py](../src/driver_monitor/state/fatigue.py), [detectors/drowsy.py](../src/driver_monitor/detectors/drowsy.py) |

Always reported (not detections, but measurements in every message): eye openness and state per eye,
blink timing, the driver's learned normal opening and the current ratio to it, head yaw/pitch/roll and
their speeds, **gaze direction label + angle + zone** (head + eyes), mouth opening, on-road / off-road,
PERCLOS, blink rate, yawn/nod/microsleep counts.

**Tired vs. not paying attention** are separate on purpose:
* *Tired* = the body is shutting down: eyelids, blink timing, yawns, the head sinking. It accumulates over minutes.
  Two patterns, both covered: **acute** (one long closure — alarmed instantly as `MICROSLEEP`, weighs heavily,
  then fades while the driver stays alert) and **chronic** (many shorter closures/long blinks over minutes —
  each light, but they stack up while they keep coming). See [8.3](#83-drowsiness-level).
* *Not paying attention* (`DISTRACTED`) = the eyes are open and working but pointed away from the road too long.
* Closed eyes are **never** counted as distraction (gaze becomes `UNKNOWN`); they go to the drowsiness path, which is more severe.

## 2. Running it and testing on your own face

```powershell
cd driver_monitor
uv sync                                   # once: installs everything into .venv
uv run python tools/download_model.py     # once: models/face_landmarker.task (3.8 MB)
uv run python -m driver_monitor           # camera window + terminal output
```

What happens:

1. **Calibration (~10 s).** The terminal and the window tell you what to do:
   * 6 s: *look straight ahead (at the screen centre), eyes normally open, keep still.* This records your neutral head pose, open-eye values and neutral gaze.
   * 3 s: *close your eyes.* This records your closed-eye values.
   * It restarts by itself if your face was not visible or your head moved too much.
2. **Monitoring.** One status line updates ~5×/s. Every time a detection starts, changes severity or ends, a coloured line is printed and stays in the history:
   ```
   [  21.0s] ▲ WARNING  EYES_CLOSED        — started: eyes closed 0.50s (microsleep in 0.5s)
   [  21.5s] ■ CRITICAL MICROSLEEP         — started: eyes closed 1.00s
   [  21.5s] ✓ ENDED    EYES_CLOSED        — 1.00s (ESCALATED_TO_MICROSLEEP)
   [  23.3s] ✓ ENDED    MICROSLEEP         — 2.47s total (1 in last 10 min)
   [  24.6s] ● INFO     DROWSY             — started: SLIGHTLY_DROWSY score 0.25 (MICROSLEEPS)
   [  26.4s] INFO     | eyes OPEN 0.97 | head y-2 p+1 r+0 | gaze LEFT moderate (-33°,-2°) 0.8s | mouth CLOSED | ...
   ```
3. **Window keys:** `q`/`Esc` quit · `c` recalibrate (resets everything) · `j` print the full JSON once per second · `m` toggle mirror.

Options: `--camera N`, `--list-cameras`, `--json session.jsonl` (save every message), `--no-window`, `--no-calibration`,
`--scroll` (print status lines instead of updating one line), `--video clip.mp4` (process a recording),
`--backend auto|msmf|dshow|any`, `--config my.yaml`, `--ws URL`, `--no-ws`, `--dump-config`, `--print-schema`.

**Settings file.** [config/driver_monitor.yaml](../config/driver_monitor.yaml) is loaded automatically at start
(`--config other.yaml` uses another file); only the keys it contains override the defaults.

**WebSocket output.** While the camera runs, every `driver_state` message (section 9) is sent as one WebSocket
text message to `websocket.url` from the settings file (default `ws://localhost:8765`; `--ws URL` overrides it,
`--no-ws` turns it off). [output/ws_sender.py](../src/driver_monitor/output/ws_sender.py) `WebSocketSender`
sends from its own thread, so the camera loop never waits for the network:

* the server may start later or restart — the sender reconnects every `reconnect_s` (2 s) and prints
  `WebSocket: connected …` / `… lost` / `cannot connect …` (once per outage) in the terminal;
* while disconnected nothing is buffered (the receiver wants the live state, not a backlog); if sending is
  slower than the camera, the oldest queued messages are dropped (`queue_size` 30);
* `max_rate_hz` limits the rate (0 = every processed frame, ~30/s);
* on exit it prints how many messages were sent and dropped.

To watch the stream without the simulator: `uv run python tools/ws_receiver.py` (prints one summary line per
second; `--full` prints every message).

**Camera and frame rate.** The default camera is index **1** (the external USB camera on the dev
machine), falling back to index 0 when it is not plugged in; `--camera N` picks one explicitly and
`--list-cameras` probes all cameras and prints the frame rate each one *really* delivers. The capture
backend defaults to `auto` = Windows Media Foundation (MSMF) first: with DirectShow most USB webcams
ignore the MJPG request and stay in raw YUY2, which USB 2.0 can only carry at **~10 fps** in 1280×720
(measured on both cameras of the dev machine; MSMF gives 30 fps). The panel's first line shows
`cam … fps | loop … fps | proc … ms`: camera rate, processed frames per second and processing time per frame
(~15–20 ms on the dev laptop). If `loop` is lower than `cam`, the CPU is the bottleneck (e.g. a screen
recorder or other heavy programs running at the same time).

### A test plan for your face

| Try this | Expect |
|---|---|
| Blink normally | Nothing (eye state flickers `CLOSED` in the status line, blink rate rises) |
| Close your eyes for ~0.7 s | `EYES_CLOSED` started → ended `REOPENED` |
| Close your eyes for 2–3 s, then drive normally | `EYES_CLOSED` → `MICROSLEEP` CRITICAL at 1 s (the instant alarm); `DROWSY` rises to `SLIGHTLY_DROWSY` and goes back to `ALERT` after a few minutes of normal driving |
| Close your eyes for ~0.8 s every 30 s for a few minutes | `EYES_CLOSED` each time; `DROWSY` climbs over minutes: `SLIGHTLY` → `DROWSY` (~3 min) → `VERY_DROWSY` (~6 min) |
| Squint / hold your eyes half shut for 3 s while looking straight | `EYES_ALMOST_CLOSED` |
| After ≥ 30 s of normal driving, keep your eyes open but clearly narrower than usual (heavy, tired eyes) for 10 s | `EYES_NARROWED` after ~5 s, `DROWSY` at `SLIGHTLY_DROWSY`; `eyes.openness_ratio` ≈ 0.7–0.85 in the JSON |
| The same while smiling broadly | Nothing (smile guard) |
| Yawn (also a natural, not fully open yawn) | `YAWN` started/ended, yawn count rises |
| Talk or laugh | No `YAWN` |
| Look at a mirror (simulator: rear-view top centre, side mirrors in the lower screen corners) | Gaze zone `REAR_MIRROR` / `LEFT_MIRROR` / `RIGHT_MIRROR`, attention `CHECKING_MIRROR`, no detection (only a stare > 3 s) |
| Look out to the side for 2–3 s | Zone `LEFT_SIDE`/`RIGHT_SIDE`, no detection (`DISTRACTED` only after 3 s) |
| Look down at your phone for 3–5 s (head and eyes down) | `DISTRACTED` `LOOKING_DOWN` WARNING at 1.2 s → DANGER at 2 s, zone `DOWN`; **no** `HEAD_FALLING`, `HEAD_NOD` or drowsiness |
| Look straight and close your eyes | `CLOSED`, gaze `UNKNOWN` after 0.5 s (not `DOWN`) |
| Many short glances down (each < 1.2 s) within 30 s | `DISTRACTED` reason `CUMULATIVE` |
| Let your head sink slowly with heavy (but open) eyes | Nothing yet; once the eyes **close** while it sinks: `HEAD_FALLING` FORWARD, CRITICAL |
| Close your eyes, drop your head quickly and lift it back | `HEAD_NOD` (with eyes open it is a glance/"yes" nod: nothing) |
| Close your eyes and tilt your head to a shoulder | `HEAD_FALLING` `SIDEWAYS_LEFT/RIGHT` |
| Turn your head far to a side window | Gaze `LEFT`/`RIGHT`, `DISTRACTED` after 3 s; **no** `HEAD_FALLING` (pose unreliable near profile) |
| Turn far away / cover the camera for 2+ s | `FACE_NOT_VISIBLE` with `likely_cause` |
| Look up, up-left, down-right … | Gaze direction label + angles in the status line |

**First check the signs** of your setup: turn your head to *your* left → head yaw must go **negative**;
look down → pitch **negative**; tilt to your right shoulder → roll **positive**. If one is inverted,
flip it in the config (`pose.yaw_sign` etc.), not in the code.

## 3. Architecture and file map

```
 camera frame ──▶ FaceTracker (MediaPipe) ──▶ FeatureExtractor ──▶ signals ──▶ windowed state + detectors ──▶ DriverState JSON
                  perception/face_tracker      perception/features   signals/     state/ + detectors/            output/
```

Per frame, in this order ([pipeline.py](../src/driver_monitor/pipeline.py), class `DriverMonitor`):

1. **Features** — landmarks → EAR per eye, MAR, iris position/visibility, blendshape values, head angles (`FeatureExtractor`).
2. **Signals** — `HeadPoseProcessor` → `EyeSignalProcessor` → `GazeEstimator` → `MouthProcessor` (smoothed, relative to calibration).
3. **Detectors and windows** — eye closure/microsleep → yawn → eyes almost closed → personal eye baseline + eyes narrowed → head nod → head falling → attention + distraction → face visibility → eye statistics + tired eyes → fatigue ledger (this frame's events) → drowsiness level.
4. **Output** — `output/builder.py` turns everything into the `DriverState` pydantic model; the terminal reporter, JSONL writer, WebSocket sender and overlay consume it.

Design rules followed everywhere:

* **One class per classifier**, each with `update(...)`, `emit()` and `reset()`; detectors never talk to MediaPipe.
* **All thresholds in `config.py`** (overridable by YAML).
* **Time from timestamps, never frame counts** (webcam FPS on Windows varies, e.g. 30 → 15 in the dark).
* **Fail-safe**: unknown is reported as unknown (`null` / `UNKNOWN`), never as "fine".
* The logic below the feature extractor is camera-free, so it is unit-tested with synthetic timelines.

## 4. Conventions

| Thing | Convention |
|---|---|
| `t` | Seconds since program start (monotonic clock). |
| Left / right | The **driver's own** left/right. Inference runs on the un-mirrored frame; only the preview is mirrored. |
| `yaw_deg` | **Negative = left, positive = right** (same as the simulator's `bearing_deg`). |
| `pitch_deg` | **Positive = up, negative = down.** |
| `roll_deg` | **Negative = toward the left shoulder, positive = toward the right shoulder.** |
| Angles | Relative to the **calibrated neutral pose** (straight at the road/screen centre). |
| `openness` | 1.0 = this driver's normal open eye, 0.0 = this driver's closed eye (up to 1.3 for wide eyes). |
| Severity | `INFO` < `WARNING` < `DANGER` < `CRITICAL` (`NONE` in the summary when nothing is active). |

The head angles come from MediaPipe's facial transformation matrix, decomposed as
R = Rz(roll)·Ry(yaw)·Rx(pitch) ([geometry.py](../src/driver_monitor/perception/geometry.py) `matrix_to_euler_deg`).
That decomposition gives +yaw = left, +pitch = down, +roll = right; the signs are converted **once**, in
`FeatureExtractor`, using `pose.yaw_sign / pitch_sign / roll_sign` (defaults −1, −1, +1).

## 5. Calibration

[calibration/calibrator.py](../src/driver_monitor/calibration/calibrator.py) `Calibrator`, [calibration/profile.py](../src/driver_monitor/calibration/profile.py) `DriverProfile`.

| Phase | Duration | What is recorded | How |
|---|---|---|---|
| `OPEN_EYES` | 6 s | neutral yaw/pitch/roll; open-eye EAR and `eyeBlink` per eye; neutral iris position per eye; neutral blendshape gaze | **medians** (natural blinks in the window are ignored) |
| `CLOSED_EYES` | 3 s (first 0.8 s ignored = reaction time) | closed-eye EAR (median of the most-closed half), closed `eyeBlink` (median of the highest half) | if the closed values are not clearly different from the open ones (driver didn't close the eyes), that eye falls back to the defaults `closed EAR = 0.4 × open`, `closed blink = open + 0.5` |

Validation: the open phase restarts if the face was visible < 70 % of the time or the head moved
(std > 6°). With `--no-calibration` population defaults are used (`status: DEFAULTS`).

Why it matters: eye shapes differ a lot between people (one person's open EAR is another's half-closed),
MediaPipe blendshapes are not probabilities (an open eye can read `eyeBlink` 0.27), and a webcam below
the face makes "straight ahead" read as looking up. Everything downstream is relative to this profile.
During calibration no detections are produced; afterwards all detectors start fresh.

## 6. Signals (per-frame measurements)

### 6.1 Features — [perception/features.py](../src/driver_monitor/perception/features.py)

`FaceTracker` runs Face Landmarker in VIDEO mode with blendshapes and the transformation matrix, tracks
up to 2 faces and keeps the **largest** (the driver; passengers/people behind are ignored).
`FeatureExtractor` converts landmarks to **pixel** coordinates (x·width, y·height, z·width — computing
ratios on normalized coordinates would distort them by the aspect ratio) and computes:

| Feature | Formula / source | Landmarks |
|---|---|---|
| EAR (3D) per eye | (‖p2−p6‖ + ‖p3−p5‖) / (2‖p1−p4‖) | right 33,160,158,133,153,144 · left 362,385,387,263,373,380 |
| `eyeBlinkLeft/Right` | blendshapes | — |
| Iris position | projection of the iris centre on the corner-to-corner axis (0..1) and perpendicular offset | 468 / 473 with corners 33–133, 362–263 |
| Iris visibility | lid gap / iris diameter | 159–145, 386–374, iris rings 469–472, 474–477 |
| MAR | mean inner-lip opening / mouth width | 82–87, 13–14, 312–317 / 78–308 |
| Blendshape gaze | `eyeLookIn/Out/Up/Down` combined per direction | — |
| Smile / squint | `mouthSmile*`, `cheekSquint*`, `eyeSquint*` | — |
| Head yaw/pitch/roll | facial transformation matrix | — |
| Face box, centre, inter-ocular distance, partially out of frame | landmarks | 1, 33, 263 |

### 6.2 Head — [signals/head.py](../src/driver_monitor/signals/head.py) `HeadPoseProcessor`

Angles minus the neutral pose, smoothed with a **One-Euro filter** (heavy smoothing when still, almost
none when moving fast → no jitter and no lag on glances), angular velocities from a smoothed derivative,
and a 12 s `History` used by the nod/fall detectors and by the face-loss cause guess.

### 6.3 Eyes — [signals/eyes.py](../src/driver_monitor/signals/eyes.py) `EyeSignalProcessor`

* **Openness per eye** = 50 % EAR-based + 50 % blendshape-based, each mapped between this driver's calibrated closed (0) and open (1) values.
* **Which eye**: when the head is turned > 20°, only the eye facing the camera is used (turning **left** shows the camera the **right** side of the face → `source: RIGHT_ONLY`). The far eye is foreshortened and its EAR is wrong.
* **Smoothing**: EMA with τ = 0.04 s — light enough to keep 100 ms blinks.
* **State with hysteresis**: `CLOSED` below 0.25 (leaves only above 0.35), `ALMOST_CLOSED` below 0.55 (leaves above 0.62), else `OPEN`. Hysteresis prevents flicker around a threshold.
* **Reliability**: eye metrics are suspended (`UNKNOWN`, `eye_metrics_reliable: false`) when |yaw| > 40°, pitch < −35° or > 30°, the face is too small (< 40 px between the eyes) or partly out of frame. A turned head must never produce a false "eyes closed".
* **Looking down** (head pitch ≤ −10° relative to neutral): the eyelids follow the eyeball down, so open eyes read openness ≈ 0.3–0.6. This **never blocks** a closure; instead, while looking down, EAR gets 80 % weight (the `eyeBlink` blendshape rises when looking down) and `CLOSED` needs openness < 0.15 (leaves above 0.25). Narrowed-but-not-closed eyes while looking down are flagged `lid_lowered_by_gaze: true`, are left out of PERCLOS / mean openness, and suppress `EYES_ALMOST_CLOSED`. Clearly closed eyes with the head down still count everywhere.
  *Why not the `eyeLookDown` blendshape?* Real recordings showed it rises to 0.3–0.6 when the eyes simply **close** (head level) and stays low (0.1–0.2) when the driver looks at a phone with the head down — the opposite of what a look-down guard needs. The old guard based on it blocked real closures (they were held at `ALMOST_CLOSED`, chopped into 0.6 s pieces and never reached `MICROSLEEP`).
* **Smile guard**: `mouthSmile` > 0.5 or `cheekSquint` > 0.4 → `smiling` (squint explained).
* **Trend**: `CLOSING` / `OPENING` when |d openness/dt| > 0.4 /s.

### 6.4 Gaze — [signals/gaze.py](../src/driver_monitor/signals/gaze.py) `GazeEstimator`

`gaze angle = head angle + eye-in-head angle`, because people often turn the head only part of the way
and do the rest with the eyes (a mirror check can be 20° of head + 12° of eyes).

* Eye-in-head **yaw** = 50 % iris position (150°/unit of eye width) + 50 % `eyeLookIn/Out` blendshapes (40°/unit), relative to calibration. Eye-in-head **pitch** = 30 % iris + 70 % `eyeLookUp/Down` (vertical iris position is weak) — but only while the eyes are clearly open (openness ≥ 0.6). With narrowed eyes (squint, half-closed, closing) `eyeLookDown` follows the **lid**, not the eyeball, and used to report "looking down" while the driver looked straight ahead; then the vertical eye angle comes from the iris position alone. Clipped to ±45°, One-Euro smoothed.
* During a blink the last eye angles are held for up to 0.5 s (a blink doesn't flicker `on_road`); after that, closed eyes → direction `UNKNOWN`.
* When eye metrics are unreliable (head turned far) gaze falls back to **head only** (`eye_*: null`).
* Labels with ±2° hysteresis: horizontal `LEFT`/`RIGHT` beyond 12°, vertical `UP` beyond 12°, `DOWN` beyond 10°; combined to `CENTER`, `LEFT`, `UP_RIGHT`, `DOWN_LEFT`, …
* `intensity` from the larger of |yaw|, |pitch|: `NONE` < 12° ≤ `SLIGHT` < 25° ≤ `MODERATE` < 45° ≤ `STRONG` < 65° ≤ `EXTREME`.
* `on_road`: |yaw| ≤ 20° and −15° ≤ pitch ≤ 15° (re-entry needs 3° margin).
* **`zone`** (`ZoneClassifier`): the three mirror boxes are checked first, then the road box, then
  `DOWN` (anything below the road box — phone, lap, console), `UP`, `LEFT_SIDE` / `RIGHT_SIDE`. A zone is
  kept until the gaze is 2° outside it. The mirror boxes live in the **`gaze_zones` config section**
  (not in the code). Defaults = a driving **simulator** screen:

  | Zone | yaw (° , − left) | pitch (° , − down) | Where on the screen |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | `REAR_MIRROR` | −12 … 12 | 8 … 25 | top centre |
  | `LEFT_MIRROR` | −38 … −12 | −25 … −3 | lower left corner |
  | `RIGHT_MIRROR` | 12 … 38 | −25 … −3 | lower right corner |

  For a real left-hand-drive car something like rear 18…45 / 3…30, left −70…−30 / −25…5, right 45…85 / −25…5
  (see the docstring of `GazeZonesConfig`). The overlay shows the live zone and gaze angles, so the boxes can
  be fitted to an actual setup by looking at each mirror.

Accuracy (honest): head angles ≈ ±3–5°, eye-in-head ≈ ±5–10° on a webcam without a per-point gaze
calibration. Beyond ~70–80° yaw MediaPipe usually loses the face → `FACE_NOT_VISIBLE` with `TURNED_AWAY_*`.

### 6.5 Mouth — [signals/mouth.py](../src/driver_monitor/signals/mouth.py) `MouthProcessor`

`jawOpen` and MAR (EMA τ 0.08 s). State `WIDE_OPEN` (MAR ≥ 0.5 or jaw ≥ 0.45), `OPEN` (MAR ≥ 0.15 or jaw ≥ 0.2), else `CLOSED`.

## 7. Detections, one by one

All episodes are managed by `EpisodeTracker` ([detectors/base.py](../src/driver_monitor/detectors/base.py)):
stable `id` (e.g. `microsleep-3`), `status` `STARTED` → `ONGOING` → `ENDED` (the ENDED message is sent
exactly once, in the frame the episode ends), `started_t` = when the underlying condition began,
`duration_s`, and severity that only **rises** within an episode (except `DROWSY`, which follows the level).

### 7.1 Blinks, `EYES_CLOSED`, `MICROSLEEP` — `EyeClosureDetector`

* A **closure** = eye state `CLOSED` continuously.
* < 0.05 s: glitch, ignored. < 0.5 s: **blink** → only statistics (`last_blink`, blink rate, mean blink duration). Literature: normal blinks are 0.1–0.4 s.
* ≥ 0.5 s: `EYES_CLOSED` (WARNING) starts, with `seconds_until_microsleep`. If the eyes reopen before 1 s it ends with `outcome: REOPENED` and counts as a **long blink** (a fatigue cue).
* ≥ 1.0 s: `EYES_CLOSED` ends with `outcome: ESCALATED_TO_MICROSLEEP` and `MICROSLEEP` (CRITICAL) starts with **the same `started_t`** (when the eyes closed). At 50 km/h, 1 s is ~14 m driven blind.
* `MICROSLEEP` ends only after the eyes stay not-closed for 0.3 s, so a brief flutter does not split one microsleep into two. Before that, any closure survives not-closed blips shorter than 0.15 s (`eye_closure.flicker_s`, landmark jitter): a long closure stays **one** closure and escalates correctly.
* Short tracking gaps (up to 2 s of `UNKNOWN`) keep the closure alive; longer → ends with `outcome: TRACKING_LOST` (and `FACE_NOT_VISIBLE` / `HEAD_FALLING` take over).
* Details: `closed_for_s`, `min_openness`, `left_openness`, `right_openness`, `head_pitch_deg`, (`confirmed_t`, `head_pitch_velocity_dps`, `head_falling`, `microsleeps_10min` for microsleeps).

### 7.2 `EYES_ALMOST_CLOSED` — `EyesAlmostClosedDetector`

Eye state `ALMOST_CLOSED` held ≥ 2 s (short blinks inside don't reset it), ends after 1 s without it.
Suppressed when the narrowing is explained: **looking down**, **smiling/laughing**, **yawning**.
Details: current/mean/min openness, thresholds, per-eye openness, `looking_down`, `smiling`.

### 7.2b `EYES_NARROWED` — `EyesNarrowedDetector` (+ `EyeBaseline`)

Heavy eyelids as an **early** tiredness sign: the eyes are open, just clearly narrower than usual — milder
than `EYES_ALMOST_CLOSED`, too mild for a fixed threshold. Everything is relative to **this driver**:

* **Personal normal** ([state/eye_baseline.py](../src/driver_monitor/state/eye_baseline.py)): learned from the
  first 30 s of clean samples after calibration (75th percentile). Clean = eyes reliably measured and not
  closed, not within 0.25 s of a closure, head within ±15°, not looking down, not smiling, not yawning.
  People often open their eyes wider while calibrating, and eye shapes differ a lot — so no threshold uses an
  absolute openness. Until the normal is learned nothing is judged. Reported as `eyes.normal_openness`,
  `eyes.normal_learned`, `eyes.openness_ratio` (3 s median of the clean samples ÷ normal).
* **Episode**: ratio < 0.85 for 5 s → `EYES_NARROWED` (INFO); ends when > 0.9 for 2 s. WARNING when the
  ratio dropped below 0.7 or the episode lasts ≥ 20 s.
* **Drowsiness**: while active, evidence `NARROWED_EYES` (0.85 → 0.3 ≈ slightly drowsy, 0.65 → 0.5 ≈ drowsy,
  +0.15 after 30 s); when it ends it goes into the fatigue ledger (0.5 per minute of narrowing, max 1.0) and
  the history (`narrowed_eyes`, `narrowed_eyes_s`), so repeated episodes stack up.
* Details: `openness_ratio`, `min_ratio`, `mean_ratio`, `normal_openness`, `normal_learned`, `narrowed_below_ratio`.
* Recording 12 (eyes at ~80 % of normal from ~49 s): `EYES_NARROWED` at 56 s, `SLIGHTLY_DROWSY` at 60 s.

### 7.3 `TIRED_EYES` — `TiredEyesDetector` (+ `EyeStatistics`)

From the last 60 s (needs ≥ 30 s of valid data): `HEAVY_EYELIDS` (mean openness of non-closed eyes < 0.8 of the driver's normal),
`SLOW_BLINKS` (mean blink > 300 ms with ≥ 3 blinks), `FREQUENT_LONG_BLINKS` (≥ 3 closures of 0.5–1 s).
INFO with one reason, WARNING with two or more; ends after 30 s without any reason.

### 7.4 `YAWN` — `YawnDetector`

A yawn is **one continuous mouth opening** (open = MAR ≥ 0.30 or `jawOpen` ≥ 0.40; dips shorter than
0.15 s do not split it) that is either

* **wide** (peak MAR ≥ 0.55 or jaw ≥ 0.65) and has lasted ≥ 0.4 s, or
* **moderate** (peak MAR ≥ 0.40 or jaw ≥ 0.50) and has lasted ≥ 1.5 s — natural yawns often do not open fully.

The whole yawn counts, including the opening and closing phases (the old rule needed ≥ 1.5 s *continuously
wide open* and missed real yawns whose wide phase lasted 0.6–0.7 s). Speech keeps opening and closing below
MAR 0.3 (measured on the test recordings), so it does not form one long opening. `jawOpen` is **ignored
while the head is pitched down** (≤ −10°): with the chin lowered it reads ~0.6 with a closed mouth.
`started_t` = when the mouth opened; ends after the mouth has been below the open level for 0.5 s;
completed yawns are counted (`yawns_5min`) and add 0.4 to the fatigue load.
Details: `jaw_open`, `max_jaw_open`, `mar`, `max_mar`, `open_s`, `head_tilt_back_deg` (the head usually tilts
back during a yawn), `jaw_ignored_head_down`, `yawns_5min`, `eyes_squeezed`.
Limitation: a hand over the mouth can hide the yawn (no fake detection).

### 7.5 `HEAD_NOD` — `HeadNodDetector`

A drop of ≥ 12° within 0.8 s at ≥ 25°/s **from a normal head position** (−8°…+8°; coming back from
looking up is not a nod; |yaw| ≤ 30°), then the head comes back by ≥ 50 % within 2 s, **and** the eyes were
**measured closed** for ≥ 0.4 s during the dip (longer than the blink that comes with every gaze shift) and
open for at most 30 % of it. A glance down at a phone lowers the lids to ~0.4–0.6 with the eyes open, and a
"yes" nod keeps them open — neither is a nod; eyes that cannot be measured never count as closed.
Reported once when the head comes back (`status: ENDED`). DANGER if the eyes were closed ≥ 0.5 s, else WARNING.
Details: start/lowest pitch, `drop_deg`, max speed, drop time, recovery time, `mean_eye_openness`,
`eyes_closed_s`, `eyes_open_fraction`, `head_nods_5min`.

### 7.6 `HEAD_FALLING` — `HeadFallingDetector`

The head sinks **with closed eyes**. The eyes are the deciding signal: looking down at a phone moves the
head 15–25° down and lowers the lids (openness ~0.5) with the eyes **open** — that is `DISTRACTED`, never
`HEAD_FALLING`. Every start condition therefore needs eyes that are **measured closed** (the eye-closure
detector's view, which also bridges short tracking gaps); "eyes unknown" never counts as closed.

| Direction / pattern | Condition |
|---|---|
| `FORWARD` `HELD_DOWN` / `SUDDEN_DROP` | pitch ≤ −15° together with closed eyes for ≥ 1 s (`SUDDEN_DROP` if it got there ≥ 12° within 0.8 s) |
| `FORWARD` `SLOW_DRIFT` | over the last 3 s the pitch fell ≥ 10° **steadily** (checked once per second, every second ≥ 2° lower) and the eyes have been closed ≥ 0.5 s |
| `SIDEWAYS_LEFT/RIGHT` | \|roll\| ≥ 20° with closed eyes for 1 s, or a steady roll drift ≥ 15° with closed eyes |
| `BACKWARD` | pitch ≥ +20° with eyes closed ≥ 1 s |

Nothing starts while |yaw| > 30°: near a profile view MediaPipe's pitch/roll are unreliable (in the test
recordings, turning to a side window produced fake "sideways slumps"). The "steady" requirement means a nod
(dip and back) or one quick move down is not a slow drift.

Severity DANGER; CRITICAL if the eyes are closed (or a microsleep is active) or the change is ≥ 30°.
Ends when the head is back within 8° of neutral (|roll| < 10°) for 1 s (`outcome: RECOVERED`), when the eyes have
been open again for 2 s (`EYES_REOPENED` — awake, the head position is then the driver's choice), or with
`outcome: FACE_LOST` if the face disappears.
Details: `direction`, `pattern`, `pitch_deg`, `roll_deg`, `start_value_deg`, `change_deg`, `velocity_dps`, `still_falling`,
**`trend`** (angle 3, 2, 1, 0 s ago), `eyes_state`, `eyes_openness`, `eyes_closed_during`, `head_falls_10min`.

### 7.7 `DISTRACTED` — `AttentionTracker` + `DistractionDetector`

Different rules per gaze **zone** ([6.4](#64-gaze--signalsgazepy-gazeestimator)): a driver has reasons to
look sideways (traffic, side roads) and at the mirrors, but **no reason to look down**.

| Reason | Rule | WARNING | DANGER |
|---|---|---|---|
| `LOOKING_DOWN` | time spent in zone `DOWN` within one glance (phone, lap, console) | ≥ 1.2 s | ≥ 2.0 s |
| `LONG_GLANCE` | one glance away from the road: sideways, up, or **staring** at a mirror | ≥ 3.0 s | ≥ 5.0 s |
| `CUMULATIVE` | weighted off-road time in the last 30 s: down ×1, sideways/up ×0.5, mirrors ×0 | ≥ 10 s | — |

* A **glance** = continuous time outside the `ROAD` zone; normal mirror checks (≲ 2 s) and short looks to the
  side never trigger anything. `attention.state` is `CHECKING_MIRROR` in a mirror zone, `GLANCING_AWAY` elsewhere.
* Ends when back on the road ≥ 1 s and the weighted cumulative time is < 8 s.
* Off-road time only accumulates while the gaze is **known**: closed eyes or a lost face neither extend nor end a glance (they are drowsiness / face-loss problems, not distraction).
* All limits and weights: `distraction.*` config; mirror positions: `gaze_zones.*`.
* Details: `reason`, `zone`, `direction`, `horizontal`, `vertical`, `intensity`, gaze and head angles of the last off-road sample, `glance_s`, `glance_down_s`, `longest_glance_s`, thresholds, `off_road_last_30s_s`, `weighted_off_road_30s_s`.

### 7.8 `FACE_NOT_VISIBLE` — `FaceVisibilityDetector`

No face for ≥ 2 s (fail-safe: "nothing detected" is never treated as "driver fine"). WARNING; DANGER
after 5 s or when the head dropped. `likely_cause` from the last 0.6 s before the loss:
`TURNED_AWAY_LEFT/RIGHT` (yaw beyond ±35° or turning > 60°/s), `HEAD_DROPPED` (pitch < −20°, dropping fast,
or `HEAD_FALLING` was active), `LEFT_FRAME` (face near the image edge), `OCCLUDED` (lost mid-image with a
normal pose — hand, object, camera covered), `UNKNOWN` (never seen). Ends when the face is back for 0.5 s.

### 7.9 `DROWSY` — `DrowsinessEstimator` + `DrowsyDetector`

See [8.3](#83-drowsiness-level). Active while the level is above `ALERT`; severity = level
(`SLIGHTLY_DROWSY` INFO, `DROWSY` WARNING, `VERY_DROWSY` DANGER, `SEVERELY_DROWSY` CRITICAL).
Details: `level`, `level_index`, `score`, `previous_level`, `level_since_t`, `contributing`, `fatigue_load`, `floor_level`, `next_possible_drop_t`.
The severity can go **down** within the episode: the level recovers when the evidence fades, and the
episode ends when the level is back at `ALERT`.

## 8. Windowed state: attention, PERCLOS, drowsiness level

### 8.1 Attention — [state/attention.py](../src/driver_monitor/state/attention.py)

`zone`, `eyes_off_road_s` (current glance), `glance_down_s` (of which down), `off_road_last_30s_s`,
`weighted_off_road_30s_s` (down ×1, sideways ×0.5, mirrors ×0), `longest_glance_60s_s`, `glances_60s`
(glances ≥ 0.2 s), `state` (`ATTENTIVE`, `CHECKING_MIRROR`, `GLANCING_AWAY`, `DISTRACTED`, `UNKNOWN`).

### 8.2 Eye statistics and PERCLOS — [state/eye_stats.py](../src/driver_monitor/state/eye_stats.py)

* **PERCLOS (P80-style)** = fraction of the last 60 s with eyes ≥ 80 % closed (openness ≤ 0.2). It is the most validated camera drowsiness metric (≈ < 0.08 alert, ≥ 0.15 drowsy).
* **Time-weighted**, not frame-counted: each sample holds until the next one; gaps > 0.3 s, missing face, head turned, looking down with not-closed eyes = **no data** (never counted as closed nor open). Clearly closed eyes with the head down do count.
* Reported only after ≥ 30 s of valid data (`warming_up` before).
* Also: mean openness of non-closed eyes, blink rate per minute, mean blink duration, long blinks.

### 8.3 Drowsiness level — [state/drowsiness.py](../src/driver_monitor/state/drowsiness.py)

Tiredness is a **trend**, not a single moment. Acute dangers are alarmed **instantly by their own
detections** (`EYES_CLOSED` at 0.5 s, `MICROSLEEP` CRITICAL at 1 s, `HEAD_FALLING`); for the drowsiness
level they are only evidence that adds up over time — and fades again when the driver is alert.

**Two patterns, both drowsiness, both covered:**

| Pattern | Example | Instant reaction | Level |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Acute** — one long closure | eyes closed 3 s once | `MICROSLEEP` CRITICAL at 1 s (until the eyes open) | heavy weight at once (DROWSY briefly, then SLIGHTLY), back to ALERT after ~5 min of normal driving |
| **Chronic** — frequent shorter closures | eyes closed 0.8 s every 30 s | `EYES_CLOSED` WARNING each time | each one light, but they stack: SLIGHTLY → DROWSY (~3 min) → VERY (~6 min) → SEVERELY (~8 min) |

**1. Fatigue ledger** ([state/fatigue.py](../src/driver_monitor/state/fatigue.py) `FatigueLedger`). Every
fatigue event from the detection stream is stored with a weight that **halves every 5 minutes**:
`load = Σ weight × 0.5^(age / 300 s)`.

| Event | Weight |
|---|---|
| eye closure ≥ 0.5 s (`EYES_CLOSED` or `MICROSLEEP`), by its duration — smooth, no cliff at 1 s | 0.5 s → 0.2 · 1 s → 0.5 · 1.5 s → 0.9 · 2 s → 1.1 · 3 s → 1.5 · 5 s+ → 2.0 |
| `HEAD_FALLING` (always with closed eyes) | 1.0 |
| `HEAD_NOD` | 0.6 |
| `YAWN` | 0.4 |
| `EYES_NARROWED` episode (added when it ends) | 0.5 per minute of narrowing, max 1.0 |

A running microsleep is updated every frame, so its weight grows while the eyes stay shut. The ledger
also provides the **history** in the JSON: per last 1 / 5 / 15 min the number of long eye closures,
microsleeps, total closed-eye time, yawns, nods, head falls and narrowed-eye episodes (+ their time).

**2. Evidence 0..1** per indicator through a curve (0.25 ≈ slight, 0.5 ≈ drowsy, 0.75 ≈ very):

| Indicator | Evidence 0 → 0.25 → 0.5 → 0.75 → 1 |
|---|---|
| PERCLOS (60 s) | 0.04 → 0.08 → 0.15 → 0.25 → 0.40 |
| mean openness (60 s) **÷ the driver's normal** | 0.85 → 0.75 → 0.65 → 0.50 → 0.35 |
| `NARROWED_EYES` (only while `EYES_NARROWED` is active; ratio to normal) | 0.85 → 0.3, 0.75 → 0.4, 0.65 → 0.5, 0.55 → 0.6; +0.15 after 30 s |
| mean blink duration (60 s) | 220 → 300 → 400 → 550 → 800 ms |
| long blinks / 60 s | 0 → 2 → 4 → 7 → 10 |
| `RECENT_EVENTS` = fatigue load | 0 → (1.0: 0.3) → (2.0: 0.55) → (3.0: 0.7) → (4.0: 0.85) |

The 60 s indicators react within a minute and fall away by themselves; the fatigue load carries the
minutes-long trend.

3. **raw score** = strongest evidence + 0.2 × sum of the others (several cues add up).
4. **score** = asymmetric EMA: rises with τ = 3 s, falls with τ = 15 s.
5. **level** from the score: ≥ 0.25 `SLIGHTLY_DROWSY`, ≥ 0.45 `DROWSY`, ≥ 0.65 `VERY_DROWSY`, ≥ 0.80 `SEVERELY_DROWSY`.
6. **Fast up, corrected down**: the level rises immediately and drops at most one step per 30 s once the
   evidence is gone. There are **no hard floors** any more (previously one microsleep forced `VERY_DROWSY`
   for 10 minutes — even when the driver was clearly alert afterwards).
7. **Fatigue memory** only after real fatigue: once the level reached `VERY_DROWSY` it never drops below
   *session peak − 2* (feeling fine for a moment, e.g. while chatting, does not erase real fatigue). One short
   event never leaves a memory.
8. `contributing`: indicators with evidence ≥ 0.25, strongest first; `RECENT_EVENTS` is expanded into the
   event kinds that make up the load (`MICROSLEEPS`, `LONG_EYE_CLOSURES`, `HEAD_NODS`, `HEAD_FALLING`, `YAWNING`,
   `NARROWED_EYES`).

Speed matters for how dangerous a closure is (2 s at 130 km/h is 72 m blind). That judgement belongs to
the consumer that knows the speed (alert manager / assistant): it gets every `MICROSLEEP` instantly with
its exact `closed_for_s`.

## 9. The JSON message

The pydantic model in [output/schema.py](../src/driver_monitor/output/schema.py) is the source of truth;
`uv run python -m driver_monitor --print-schema` prints the JSON Schema (for the simulator team).

```
driver_state
├─ type: "driver_state", schema_version: 1, t, frame
├─ summary       { highest_severity, primary, active[] }
├─ quality       { fps, camera_fps, latency_ms, face_detected, faces_in_view, face_lost_for_s, eye_metrics_reliable, issues[] }
├─ calibration   { status, phase, progress, message, neutral_head{yaw_deg,pitch_deg,roll_deg}, open_ear{left,right} }
├─ eyes          { left{openness,ear,blink_score,state}, right{…}, openness, state, state_for_s, trend,
│                  openness_velocity_per_s, source, lid_lowered_by_gaze, last_blink{ended_t,duration_ms},
│                  normal_openness, normal_learned, openness_ratio }
├─ head          { yaw_deg, pitch_deg, roll_deg, yaw_velocity_dps, pitch_velocity_dps, roll_velocity_dps }
├─ gaze          { direction, horizontal, vertical, intensity, yaw_deg, pitch_deg, head_yaw_deg, head_pitch_deg,
│                  eye_yaw_deg, eye_pitch_deg, on_road, direction_for_s, zone, zone_for_s }
├─ mouth         { jaw_open, mar, state, state_for_s }
├─ attention     { state, zone, eyes_off_road_s, glance_down_s, off_road_last_30s_s, weighted_off_road_30s_s,
│                  longest_glance_60s_s, glances_60s }
├─ drowsiness    { level, level_index, score, peak_level, warming_up, contributing[], fatigue_load,
│                  history[ { window_s (60/300/900), long_eye_closures, microsleeps, closed_eyes_s,
│                             yawns, head_nods, head_falls, narrowed_eyes, narrowed_eyes_s } ],
│                  indicators{ perclos, perclos_window_s, blink_rate_per_min, mean_blink_duration_ms,
│                              long_blinks_60s, mean_openness_60s, mean_openness_ratio_60s, yawns_5min,
│                              head_nods_5min, head_falls_10min, microsleeps_10min } }
└─ detections[]  { id, type, severity, status, started_t, ended_t, duration_s, details{…type-specific…} }
```

* **Three layers**: signals (what is measured now) → windowed state (what happened over seconds/minutes) → detections (labelled episodes).
* **A state is not a detection**: `eyes.state = CLOSED` during a 150 ms blink is just a measurement.
* `quality.issues`: `CALIBRATING`, `HEAD_TURNED_TOO_FAR`, `FACE_TOO_SMALL`, `FACE_PARTIALLY_OUT_OF_FRAME`, `LOW_LIGHT` (mean gray < 50), `LOW_FPS` (< 15).
* `quality.fps` = frames processed per second, `quality.camera_fps` = frames the camera really delivers (`null` for video files), `quality.latency_ms` = processing time of the frame.
* `summary.primary` = most severe active detection (ties → most recent); `detections` are sorted active-first, most severe first.
* `null` always means "not measurable now" (e.g. eye values when the face is lost).
* Transport: the same message goes to the WebSocket (`websocket.url`, one text message each, see section 2)
  and, with `--json`, to a JSON Lines file.
* Messages are produced every frame; `--json` writes all of them (JSON Lines). A consumer that samples
  less often should watch `STARTED`/`ENDED` statuses or keep the latest message per detection `id`.

**Field-by-field reference** of every block and every detection's `details`, for consumers such as the
simulator: [detection-schema.md](detection-schema.md). Machine-readable JSON Schema:
[driver_state.schema.json](driver_state.schema.json) (a test fails when it no longer matches the code).

## 11. Configuration

Every number above lives in [config.py](../src/driver_monitor/config.py). Print all of them with
`uv run python -m driver_monitor --dump-config > my.yaml`, edit, and run with `--config my.yaml`
(only the keys you keep are overridden; unknown keys are rejected so typos are caught). Sections:
`camera`, `landmarker`, `pose`, `calibration`, `eyes`, `eye_closure`, `almost_closed`, `eye_baseline`,
`narrowed_eyes`, `tired_eyes`, `mouth`, `yawn`, `nod`, `head_falling`, `gaze`, `gaze_zones`, `distraction`,
`face`, `drowsiness`, `quality`, `output`, `websocket`. The file [config/driver_monitor.yaml](../config/driver_monitor.yaml)
is loaded automatically (it holds the WebSocket address); `--config` replaces it with another file.

Example — stricter microsleep, a wider on-road box and the mirrors of a triple-monitor simulator (mirror
insets on the side screens):

```yaml
eye_closure:
  microsleep_s: 0.8
gaze:
  on_road_yaw_deg: 35.0
gaze_zones:
  left_mirror:  { yaw_min_deg: -60.0, yaw_max_deg: -38.0, pitch_min_deg: -20.0, pitch_max_deg: 0.0 }
  right_mirror: { yaw_min_deg: 38.0,  yaw_max_deg: 60.0,  pitch_min_deg: -20.0, pitch_max_deg: 0.0 }

  down_warning_s: 1.0
```

## 12. Tests

```powershell
uv run pytest          # synthetic timelines, every scenario end-to-end, real recordings (~15 min)
uv run ruff check .    # lint
uv run mypy            # strict type check of src/
```

* `tests/test_eye_closure.py` — blink vs. long blink vs. microsleep, escalation keeps the start time, flutter does not split, tracking gaps, episode lifecycle.
* `tests/test_mouth_and_eyes.py` — talking is not a yawn, slightly open mouth is not a yawn, `jawOpen` ignored with the head down, wide yawn timing, natural moderate yawn, heavy eyelids, blink inside heavy eyelids, look-down/smile/yawn guards.
* `tests/test_head.py` — nod with closed eyes vs. open eyes vs. glance down with lowered lids vs. coming back from looking up; slow drift with closed eyes; phone look-down with lowered lids is not falling; closed eyes → CRITICAL; no sideways fall with the head turned far.
* `tests/test_schema_file.py` — `docs/driver_state.schema.json` still matches the pydantic model.
* `tests/test_ws_sender.py` — against a real local WebSocket server: complete driver_state messages arrive, a missing server never blocks the caller and the sender connects once the server appears, the rate limit.
* `tests/test_narrowed_eyes.py` — narrowing is judged relative to the driver's own learned normal (narrow, average and wide eyes alike), normal variation / short narrowing / looking down / smiling never trigger, WARNING for strong or long narrowing, slightly drowsy while narrowed then recovery, episodes enter the fatigue history.
* `tests/test_attention_and_state.py` — simulator mirror checks (and many of them) are not distraction, looking down is judged much faster than sideways, long look down WARNING→DANGER, closed eyes ≠ distraction, cumulative short looks down, face-loss cause, time-weighted PERCLOS with gaps, a single event is corrected and leaves no memory, fatigue memory only after a high level, PERCLOS alone → DROWSY.
* `tests/test_fatigue.py` — closure weight grows with duration without a cliff at 1 s, half-life decay and 1/5/15 min history, an escalated closure is counted once, long blinks stack up.
* `tests/test_scenarios.py` — all scenarios of section 10 with their expected detections (regression guard), incl. the acute (`recovering`) and chronic (`long_blinks_trend`) drowsiness patterns.
* `tests/test_recordings.py` — **real webcam recordings** (see below): closures → `MICROSLEEP`, phone look-downs → `DISTRACTED` without `HEAD_FALLING`/`HEAD_NOD`, natural yawns found, looking around is not drowsiness, one closure → at most `SLIGHTLY_DROWSY`, a look down flagged after ~1.2 s (r10), narrowed eyes → `EYES_NARROWED` (r12). Skipped when the cached recordings are missing.

**Real recordings as regression tests.** `recordings/` (git-ignored — it shows a real person) holds test
videos and, next to each, an `.npz` with the cached MediaPipe output (landmarks, blendshapes, pose
matrix per frame). The test replays that cache through the *current* feature and detector code, so it is
fast and needs no camera. To add a recording:

```powershell
uv run python tools/cache_recording.py recordings/clip.mp4            # a raw camera video
uv run python tools/cache_recording.py recordings/rec.mp4 --screen    # a screen capture of the preview window
```

`--screen` crops the camera area out of a screen recording, un-mirrors it and scales it to 1280×720
(`--crop Y0,Y1,X0,X1` if the window was elsewhere). Then add the expected outcome to `tests/test_recordings.py`.

## 13. Limitations and tuning

* **Mirror zones are estimates** of a simulator screen layout (`gaze_zones`). Look at each mirror once with the overlay open and copy the shown gaze angles into the config; a "look at the mirrors" calibration step could do this automatically later.
* **Eye-size independence**: eye openness is normalised by the calibration (closed → 0, open → 1) and narrowing is judged against the normal opening learned while driving, so no threshold depends on someone's eye size. Until that normal is learned (~30 s of clean samples) `EYES_NARROWED` is not judged.
* **Thresholds are starting points**, checked on 11 recordings of one person (see section 12), not on many drivers. Record sessions with `--json`, look at the values, and adjust the YAML. The most person-dependent ones: `eyes.closed_enter/almost_enter`, `yawn.open_mar/wide_mar`, `gaze.*_gain_deg`, `drowsiness.closure_weight_curve/load_curve`.
* **Eye-in-head gaze is coarse** (±5–10°). Good enough for direction labels and glance timing; for "did the driver look at *this* object on the screen" a per-point screen calibration (look at targets) would be the next step.
* **Sunglasses / strong glare** hide the eyes; MediaPipe then guesses (usually "open") → missed closures rather than false alarms. Head-based detections still work.
* **Low light** → lower FPS and noisier landmarks (`LOW_LIGHT`, `LOW_FPS` issues). An IR camera is the robust fix.
* **Hand over the mouth** during a yawn → not counted.
* **Looking down vs. falling asleep** is decided by the head pitch and *measured* eye closure (never by `eyeLookDown`, which reacts to closing lids). A downward glance with the **eyes only** (head level) lowers the lids too; if it goes very far it can read as closed. In the test recordings looking down always came with ≥ 10° of head pitch. A per-driver "look down" calibration step would remove this ambiguity.
* **Low frame rate** makes blink timing coarse (a blink is 1–3 frames at 10 fps). Use a camera/backend that delivers 30 fps (`--list-cameras`) and avoid heavy programs (e.g. screen recorders) running at the same time.
* **Calibration matters**: without it (`DEFAULTS`) eye openness and angles are less accurate. Recalibrate with `c` after moving the camera or changing seats.
* `c` (recalibrate) resets all detectors and the fatigue memory.
